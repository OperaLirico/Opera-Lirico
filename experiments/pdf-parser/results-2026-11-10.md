## Findings

1. Docling successfully processed these born-digital/text-bearing samples, preserving page provenance across all pages.
2. Table extraction worked on one 8-page conservatory notice, yielding five tables. This still needs manual comparison against the original tables.
3. Keyword matching is not semantic classification. “Basso” in “basso tuba” is an instrument, not a contralto/bass singer. The evaluation script now separates voice-term candidates, vocal context and instrument context.
4. The 37-page sample took 110.27 seconds, so batch size and per-document time limits matter.
5. These samples do not establish OCR accuracy on scanned PDFs. A confirmed scanned official notice is still required.
6. The result does not verify publisher identity or digital signatures. SHA-256 only confirms that the local bytes match the manifest.

## Decision

Keep Docling isolated. It is a promising candidate for page-aware PDF text/table extraction, but it is not ready to replace production parsing or generate publishable opportunity fields. Next validation should compare page-level output against a manually annotated ground-truth set, including at least one confirmed scanned PDF and one vocal recruitment PDF with a known deadline, age/eligibility section and repertoire table.
