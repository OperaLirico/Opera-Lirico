# Masterclass editorial standards

## Audience
Chinese-speaking voice students, conservatory students, early-career singers, répétiteurs, and professional singers looking for courses in Europe.

## Writing principles
- Write idiomatic Chinese, not sentence-by-sentence translation from Italian or English.
- Put the teacher and learning subject first. Keep the original event title in a separate secondary line for source matching.
- Prefer concrete terms used by singers: 发声、共鸣、声音投射、呼吸、咬字、风格、角色诠释、合作钢琴、艺术指导.
- Avoid stiff wording and vague claims such as "提升综合能力".
- Explain what participants will study, who can apply, what repertoire is expected, and whether there is a final performance.
- Preserve names of people, institutions, venues, and official repertoire titles accurately. Use established Chinese opera titles where clear; retain the original title when ambiguity is possible.
- Do not invent course outcomes, credentials, age limits, places, dates, fees, or application status.
- Separate formal-student and auditor fees; distinguish per-lesson prices from total course fees.
- If details are not published, say "尚未公布" and do not imply registration is open.
- Keep descriptions concise (normally 60–180 Chinese characters); put complex eligibility and fee details in dedicated fields.
- Use a calm, useful editorial tone. No promotional hype or machine-translated wording.

## Required display fields
Chinese display title, original title, teacher, focus/repertoire, dates, location, fee label, application deadline/status, organizer, official source, and last verified time.
